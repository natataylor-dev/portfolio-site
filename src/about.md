Ecommerce & Marketing Manager: none of them mention 'creative
direction', 'content designer' or 'copywriter', but look at what
actually filled those roles, and the thread is clear.

At Calendar Club, The Range, Emile et Rose and Wild Things Gifts, and
through a run of freelance consultancy work in between, I've built
tone-of-voice guidelines from a blank page more than once, personally
signed off every piece of customer-facing copy against brand
standards, mapped user journeys to strip friction out of confusing
sign-up flows, and owned content governance across ecommerce
catalogues running into the thousands of SKUs. That's content design
work. It just never had the label.

The breadth is real too. I've planned and delivered integrated
strategy across email, paid search, PR and social; managed teams and
budgets (a team of 15 at The Range, a six-figure media budget at
Calendar Club); built and launched Shopify sites from scratch for
freelance clients; and, earlier in my career, worked hands-on as a
graphic designer and production assistant before moving client-side
into digital marketing in 2006. I still do the visual work myself in
Canva, InDesign, Illustrator and Photoshop when a project calls for
it, which means the content and design decisions on a project rarely
get made in isolation from each other.

## How I work

<div class="pull-quote">
  <p class="quote-label">direction matters</p>
  <blockquote>
    "No wind favors he who has no destined port."
    <cite>– Seneca</cite>
  </blockquote>

Most of the work on this site was produced inside small, resource-lean
teams – often without dedicated content tooling, formal research
budgets, or a content design function to sit inside. That's shaped how
I work: efficient, collaborative, comfortable with ambiguity, and
focused on getting good content shipped rather than running a perfect
process.
If you're looking for someone who's only ever worked inside a mature
content design system, this isn't quite that. If you want someone who
can build the thinking from scratch, under real constraints, and still
land on something that measurably works – that's what the case studies
here are trying to show.

Twenty years in, that's still the thread: knowing what a piece of
content, a campaign or a career move is actually for, before worrying
about how polished it looks getting there.
