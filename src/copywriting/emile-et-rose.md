## Key wins

<div class="skills-grid">
  <div class="skill-card">
    <h3>One tone, three campaign types</h3>
    <p>Held a warm, sweet, never-intrusive brand voice consistently across seasonal, promotional and craft-led emails.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="skill-card">
    <h3>Matched the copy to the job</h3>
    <p>Playful wordplay for a light seasonal touchpoint, minimal copy for a discount-led flash sale, product detail for a collection launch.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="skill-card">
    <h3>Understood what 'success' meant per email</h3>
    <p>Recognised that engagement, conversion and brand presence are different goals, and didn't judge every send against the same metric.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<a href="https://www.emile-et-rose.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Emile et Rose</a> is a traditional babywear brand based in Devon,
with a tone of voice that's warm, friendly, respectful and sweet –
never too intrusive. The audience is largely women, and the brand's
classic British style attracts an affluent demographic. Email had to
hold that tone consistently across three different jobs: seasonal
launches, promotional campaigns, and deep dives on the quality and
craftsmanship of the garments themselves.

<div class="sample-section">

<p class="eyebrow">Seasonal launch</p>
<h3>Easter Shop</h3>

<p class="case-skills"><span class="eyebrow-label">Objective:</span>
launch the Easter shop with seasonal, on-brand playfulness, while
keeping the tone gentle rather than pushy</p>
<p class="case-skills"><span class="eyebrow-label">Call to
action:</span> hop over to the Easter Shop</p>

A seasonal launch email for the brand's Easter collection, leaning
into light wordplay ('hop', 'chick', 'egg-stra') while staying within
the brand's warm, sweet, never-intrusive tone of voice.

> **Subject: Hoppy Days are Just Ahead 🐰 Spring into Action 🌼**

</div>

<div class="sample-section">

<p class="eyebrow">Seasonal launch</p>
<h3>AW23 Collection</h3>

<p class="case-skills"><span class="eyebrow-label">Objective:</span>
launch the new seasonal collection and draw attention to garment
craftsmanship and detailing, rather than price or promotion</p>
<p class="case-skills"><span class="eyebrow-label">Call to
action:</span> shop AW23 now</p>

A collection launch email built around fabric and construction detail
– pintuck pleats, tulle, embroidery, checks – letting the quality of
the garments themselves carry the copy rather than a seasonal hook.

<div class="sample-section">

<p class="eyebrow">Promotion</p>
<h3>Flash Sale</h3>

<p class="case-skills"><span class="eyebrow-label">Objective:</span>
clear remaining Autumn/Winter stock with a short, high-urgency
discount window, ahead of the next season's launch</p>
<p class="case-skills"><span class="eyebrow-label">Call to
action:</span> shop now</p>

A weekend flash sale that typically closes out a season. Deliberately
light on copy – limiting the text was part of the strategy, not an
afterthought – so the email hits only what actually drives action: the
discount itself, the styles it applies to, and how long it runs for.

> **Subject: Flash Sale Weekend | 50% off Autumn/Winter 2022 Styles**
>
> This weekend, our 2022 Autumn/Winter Collection* is 50% off.
>
> New Autumn/Winter styles now 50% off, including bestsellers Coco,
> Cooper, Clara and Connor. Shop now and receive Free Standard
> Delivery on every order delivered to a UK address. Sale ends
> midnight Monday.

Flash sales like this one played a different role again: short,
urgent and discount-led, timed deliberately to close out a season
rather than run throughout it, so they didn't compete with or
undercut the full-price launch moments above.

Collection launches like the AW23 email consistently drove high
click-through – customers wanted to look – but low immediate
conversion, since new seasons are launched ahead of the weather that
suits them: Autumn/Winter in late summer of the previous year,
Spring/Summer in late January or early February. Success here looked
different to a promo email: strong engagement and intent, with
revenue converting later as the season actually arrived, rather than
an immediate sales spike.

The Easter email sits in a different bracket again – a lighter,
lower-stakes seasonal touchpoint whose job was simply to keep the
brand present and on-tone between the harder-working promo and launch
moments, a role that doesn't show up cleanly in performance data
either.
